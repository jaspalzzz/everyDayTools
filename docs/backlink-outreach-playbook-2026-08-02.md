# Backlink outreach playbook — 2 August 2026

## Objective

Earn editorial links to the source-linked [US final paycheck law dataset](https://mypayrights.com/research/us-final-paycheck-laws), not generic links to the homepage. The asset covers 50 states and Washington, DC, separates termination and resignation timing, links every row to an official authority, offers a permanent CSV, and permits reuse under CC BY 4.0 with attribution.

Backlinks are not guaranteed. The practical goal is to create a resource that deserves citation, put it in front of people maintaining closely related pages, and measure whether the outreach produces editorial links, referral traffic, and research-page engagement.

## Deliverables

- Upgraded research page with computed findings and an accessible distribution chart.
- Permanent CSV at https://mypayrights.com/research/us-final-paycheck-laws.csv.
- Citation and CC BY 4.0 reuse guidance.
- Expanded press page with ready-to-cite data and media contact.
- Ranked prospect workbook containing exact opportunity pages, public contact routes, scoring, personalized openings, and 15 complete drafts.
- Monthly monitoring workbook plus the repository snapshot workflow below.

## First-wave order

Send only five messages initially:

1. Workplace Fairness — its wage-and-hour hub already has an exact final-paycheck resource slot.
2. FindLaw — its national table was last updated in December 2024 and has a clear downloadable-data gap.
3. Fit Small Business — its October 2024 employer guide has a concrete refresh opportunity.
4. LawInfo — its table directly matches the dataset and could cite the permanent CSV.
5. AFL-CIO — its broad worker-rights guide contains job-loss and wage-theft sections but dates to 2013.

Wait three business days before the second wave. If the first messages produce replies, incorporate the language and asset formats that resonated before sending more.

## Sending rules

- Read the exact opportunity page immediately before sending.
- Use the organization-specific opening in the workbook; remove anything that is no longer accurate.
- Send from a real @mypayrights.com mailbox with Jaspal Singh's signature.
- Link the canonical research page and CSV. Do not attach files unless requested.
- Ask for editorial consideration or a custom cut; never demand a link.
- Send one follow-up after seven days. Stop after two total touches.
- Stop immediately on a decline or removal request.
- Do not buy links, trade links, use automated bulk email, or offer disguised guest posts.
- Do not describe the dataset as legal advice, an official government publication, or attorney-reviewed.

## Personalization pattern

1. Name the exact article or resource.
2. Identify one genuine gap: old update date, no download, no primary-source links, or no national comparison.
3. Explain the relevant asset in one sentence.
4. Offer one useful format: CSV, chart, state extract, or regional cut.
5. Make a single low-pressure request.

## Monthly monitoring workflow

Use the same collection day every month:

1. In Google Search Console, record the total external links and count of top linking sites.
2. In analytics, record referral sessions and sessions to /research/us-final-paycheck-laws.
3. In the outreach workbook, count messages sent, replies, links won, and lost links.
4. Add one row to data/backlink-monitor.csv. Leave unknown values blank; never enter estimates.
5. Run npm run audit:backlinks.
6. Review reports/backlink-monitor.json for changes, response rate, link-win rate, and stale snapshots.
7. For each won link, add its exact URL to the workbook and verify that it is indexable, relevant, and not marked nofollow or sponsored. A useful nofollow citation can still send referral traffic, so log it rather than rejecting it.

Search Console's Links report is delayed and is not a complete real-time backlink index. Use it consistently for trend measurement and supplement it with direct referral traffic and the outreach log.

## Decision thresholds

Review after 25 carefully personalized messages:

- If there are replies but no links, improve the requested placement or offer a more tailored state extract.
- If there are no replies, revisit sender credibility, subject lines, and prospect/contact fit before expanding the list.
- If links are won but referral engagement is weak, prioritize pages closer to workers' immediate questions.
- If journalists reuse findings, publish the requested regional or state comparison before starting another broad campaign.

The campaign should optimize for relevant editorial citations and qualified referral visits, not raw link count.
